# Day 58 — Learning Journal

## Topic
Exploratory Testing

## What I Learned

Today, I learned that exploratory testing combines learning about an application, designing tests, and executing them. Unlike strictly scripted testing, exploratory testing allows observations from one action to guide the next action.

I also learned that effective exploratory testing is not random clicking. It requires a clear goal, relevant investigation, careful observation, and documentation.

## Practice Activities

I worked through simulated scenarios involving:

- Investigating a shopping cart total that may not update after a quantity change.
- Checking whether cart contents should persist after refreshing a page.
- Investigating the possibility of duplicate orders after rapid submissions.
- Identifying the information required in an exploratory testing report.

## Key Lessons

- Unexpected behavior is a reason to investigate, not automatic proof of a defect.
- Expected behavior should be checked against requirements.
- Repeating steps helps establish whether behavior is consistent.
- Evidence and clear documentation make findings more useful.
- A tester should not invent a root cause without sufficient evidence.

## Reflection

I improved my understanding of how exploratory testing differs from random clicking and why a focused testing objective matters. I also practiced deciding what to investigate next when unexpected behavior occurs.
