Topic: Test Closure & Lessons Learned

What I Learned

Today, I learned how QA teams formally conclude testing activities and document the results of a testing project.

I understood that test closure is more than simply finishing test execution. It involves reviewing results, documenting defects, communicating outstanding risks, preparing closure deliverables, and recording lessons learned.

Key Concepts Covered

* Purpose of test closure.
* Test closure reports.
* Test completion versus release approval.
* Test closure criteria.
* Test closure deliverables.
* Test environment cleanup and handover.
* Lessons-learned documentation.
* Final test execution metrics.
* Outstanding defects and release risks.

Practical Work

I worked through a simulated e-commerce testing scenario.

The sample project contained:

* 100 planned test cases.
* 95 executed test cases.
* 85 passed test cases.
* 10 failed test cases.
* 5 unexecuted test cases.
* 2 unresolved high-severity defects.

I calculated:

* Execution coverage: 95%.
* Pass rate among executed tests: 89.47%.

I also prepared a sample test closure summary, reviewed outstanding defects, documented lessons learned, and completed a test closure checklist.

Important Lesson

One of my most important takeaways is that test completion does not automatically mean release approval.

A QA team may finish executing all planned test cases while serious defects remain unresolved.

For example, an unresolved critical defect affecting checkout should be documented and escalated for a formal release decision.

QA must communicate the risks rather than hide defects or mark them as resolved without evidence.

Challenges

The main area I focused on was distinguishing completed testing activities from pending closure and release decisions.

I also reinforced the importance of calculating execution coverage and pass rate using the correct denominators.

Personal Reflection

Today’s lesson helped me understand the importance of professional QA reporting.

Finding defects is only part of the testing process. A QA tester must also communicate what was tested, what passed, what failed, what remains unresolved, and what risks need attention.

I now have a clearer understanding of how testing activities should be concluded and how lessons from one project can improve future testing work.
